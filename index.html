Learning Robot Learning

Concepts, pictures, and code for ETH Zürich’s Robot Learning course

This site is an independent, unofficial companion to ETH Zürich’s Robot Learning: From Fundamentals to Foundation Models, taught by Oier Mees in Spring 2026. The course page has the lecture recordings, slides, and homework.

Each course week has a page with the big picture, intuitions, the key equations explained term by term, worked NumPy examples, interactive figures, a short animation of the week’s key idea, a PyTorch sketch of the core algorithm, and summaries of the papers and the guest talk.

The course in one picture

The course moves from the robot itself to the models that are starting to control it (Figure 1). The first weeks set up the robot and the language for its decisions, then the two ways a robot can learn behaviour: by copying demonstrations and by trial and error. The middle of the course is the architectures of the foundation-model era: generative models, transformers and world models. The last weeks put them together into generalist, reasoning policies, and ask what is still missing.

How to use this site

Each week’s page is laid out the same way:

  1. Big picture: where the week fits, its key ideas and its vocabulary.
  2. Intuition: a concrete story and pictures before any math, with ▶ links to the moment in the lecture video where each idea is taught.
  3. Key concepts & formulae: the equations, in the course’s notation, each explained in words and term by term.
  4. Worked examples: the ideas applied to real numbers.
  5. Interactive: figures you can change.
  6. Algorithm & code: the NumPy code that produced the page’s figures, beside a PyTorch sketch of how you would write it at scale.
  7. Papers and Guest spotlight: original summaries of the papers discussed in class and of the guest talk.
  8. Solved problems, Recap and Cheat sheet: try each problem before opening its solution.

Big picture and Intuition are written to stand alone: reading just those two sections of every week gives a tour of the whole course. Week 12 is shorter, since it has no lecture.

The pages never reproduce the course’s slides or homework solutions; the course page is the place for those.

Three reference pages collect material from across the site:

  • Papers: every paper card, grouped by week.
  • Glossary: every symbol and term, grouped by week, with a table of symbols that change meaning.
  • Cheat sheets: every week’s key equations in one place.

For the decision-making theory underneath Weeks 2 to 5, the sibling ADM Companion goes deeper.
